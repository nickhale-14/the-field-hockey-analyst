# Analysis: what is built, what it found, what is not built

Read when working on analysis, the model, or content that cites numbers.

- **Premier Division power rankings (`analysis/prem_elo.py`, 2026-09-28)** — the Elo the user actually wanted, and the fix for why the old one was distrusted: restricting the pool to ONE division removes the failure mode entirely (a Division 1 side that never plays a Premier side can't drift above real Premier teams). Per gender, over every Prem match 2019/20-now. Margin-of-victory damped update, season-boundary regression toward the mean, promoted sides seeded below it, plus an **ordered-logit layer** that turns a rating gap into real P(home)/P(draw)/P(away) — Elo alone gives one expected score and hockey has draws. Parameters grid-searched on 2024/25 and tested once on 2025/26: **men 66.9% of results right, log-loss 0.759; women 64.4%, log-loss 0.828** (Poisson benchmark 66.1% / ~0.93, flat guess 1.099). So accuracy is a wash against the Poisson model. (This entry used to say Elo's probabilities were "much better calibrated"; that compared Prem-only Elo with an all-tier Poisson figure and doesn't hold Prem against Prem, see the 2026-11-07 comparison below.) Like the Poisson model it never picks a draw (83% accuracy on decisive men's matches; the ~18% of games that are drawn are automatic misses). Two parameters corroborate the Poisson findings independently: the women's game needs a fast rating (k 32 vs men's 12 — squad turnover) and has a far smaller home edge (15 Elo points vs 50, matching x1.07 vs x1.15 on scoring; **superseded 2026-10-20: home is 0 in both, see the calibration below**). `--rankings [--as-of DATE]` prints the weekly table with movement in the last match and across the season, both measured within-season so the between-season regression never reads as form. Reports: `analysis/reports/power_rankings_{men,women}_2026-09-28.txt`. **The top five men's ratings are exactly the big five** — an independent confirmation of the tier finding in `prem_tiers.py`.

Only two summers measurable (no 2022/23 squads), about 20 sides per gender.
- **Home advantage: none measurable in the Premier Division, clear below it (`analysis/home_advantage.py`, 2026-10-20; `reports/home_advantage_2026-10-20.txt`).** League games 2019/20-now, Finals Weekend, finals pools and play-offs excluded. Prem home goal difference per game **men +0.07 (95% interval -0.20 to +0.35), women +0.14 (-0.06 to +0.35)**; home sides win **51%** of decisive games in both (47-55%). The paired test (same two teams at both grounds in a season, ~170 pairs per gender) puts the venue at **men +0.03 (-0.17 to +0.24), women -0.01 (-0.21 to +0.18)**, permutation p 0.77 / 0.91. No season is clearly non-zero; the last three men's seasons are all slightly negative. **Division 1 and the Conferences do have one**: men +0.39 to +0.44, women +0.18 to +0.29 a game, all intervals clear of zero, home sides winning 55-57% of decisive games. So the earlier "+0.38 / +0.19" and the predictor's fitted ×1.146 / ×1.071 are lower-tier numbers (they dominate the pooled data, about 4 games in 5). **Acted on (same day): the Poisson model now fits a home edge per tier for men** (`fit_model(tier_home=True)`, `TUNED['M']['tier_home']`). 2025/26 backtest (`reports/backtest_tier_home_2026-10-20.txt`): men's fitted edges Prem x1.02, Division 1 x1.15, Conference x1.20; **Prem log-loss 0.776 -> 0.760**, accuracy 66.1% -> 65.3% (one game in 118), lower tiers unchanged. **Women's left on one shared edge**: with its ~1-year memory the per-tier fit chased noise (Prem x1.20, log-loss 0.823 -> 0.834). Moved three men's Round 5/6 likeliest scores one goal towards the away side (East Grinstead v Cardiff & Met 2-1 -> 2-2, Oxted v Cardiff & Met 3-1 -> 2-1, Old Georgians v Bowdon 7-0 -> 6-0). `predictor.py --tier-home on|off` overrides for a backtest. Elo is unaffected in practice: it is Prem-only and its home lean is learned from Prem results. A good The Finding post: "Home advantage? Not in the Prem."
- **One combined call: a 50/50 blend of the two models is at least as good as either alone (`analysis/blend_backtest.py`, 2026-10-20; `reports/blend_backtest_2026-10-20.txt`).** Prem, out of sample, 118 games per gender in 2025/26. Blend = average of Elo's and the score model's home/draw/away chances; scoreline = the score model's likeliest score *in which the blended pick wins* (as simulate.py does), so score and pick never disagree. Log-loss: **men score 0.760, Elo 0.761, 50/50 blend 0.750; women 0.823, 0.828, 0.821** (on the recalibrated Elo, 2026-10-20: Elo 0.761 / 0.833, blend 0.743 / 0.822). Accuracy moves by a game or two either way (men 65.3 / 67.8 / 66.9%, women 66.1 / 64.4 / 64.4%), which is noise at n=118. Choosing the weight on 2024/25 picked 1.0 Elo for men, but that is Elo's own tuning season, so it flatters Elo; 0.5 is the honest default. The models backed different teams in 16 (men) and 11 (women) games: score model right 5 and 6, Elo 8 and 4. Blend calibration: picks under 55% were right about half the time (49%, both genders), 85%+ picks 92% / 89%. Exact scores barely change (men 10 -> 8, women 8 -> 8 of 118) since the combined call never names a draw. Gain is small statistically; the real gain is one call per game with no contradictions, at no cost. **Predates the Elo recalibration (commit 2b592c1, same day): re-run `blend_backtest.py` against the new Elo before the combined call goes live** (its own table moves women's 2025/26 Elo log-loss 0.828 -> 0.833, men's unchanged, so the conclusion likely holds).
- **Elo v Poisson v 50/50 blend over four seasons (`analysis/compare_models.py`, 2026-11-07; `reports/model_comparison_2026-11-07.txt`).** Prem, out of sample, recalibrated Elo, 2023/24 to 2026/27 so far, 395 Men's/Open and 396 Women's games. Log-loss **Men's/Open Elo 0.675, Poisson 0.705, blend 0.674; Women's 0.834, 0.827, 0.824**. Correct: Men's/Open 290 / 286 / 291, Women's 251 / 257 / 252. Elo v Poisson is not decisive in either division (Men's/Open -0.029, 95% -0.064 to +0.008; Women's +0.008, -0.014 to +0.029). The one clear gap is blend over Poisson in the Men's/Open (+0.031, +0.015 to +0.046); Elo v blend is level there, and the Women's blend v Elo just misses (+0.010, -0.001 to +0.021). 2024/25 is both models' tuning season and Elo's best Men's/Open margin; without it, roughly Men's/Open 0.689 / 0.706 / 0.682, Women's 0.848 / 0.834 / 0.835. 2026/27 alone (no tuning, about 41 games each): Men's/Open 0.671 / 0.704 / 0.679, Women's 0.774 / 0.731 / 0.742. **Reading: the blend is the safe default, never clearly worse than either model; Elo leans better in the Men's/Open and Poisson in the Women's, so "Elo is better calibrated" is not a reason for the simulator's design.** Draws said v happened: Men's/Open Elo 44, Poisson 50, actual 55; Women's 79, 73, actual 68. **Draws in close games (`reports/draw_rate_by_gap_2026-11-07.txt`, in sample):** a draw is never the likeliest result (the translator caps it at about 24.5%), and the record agrees. Men's/Open games under a 25-point gap drew 13% (9 of 70, model 24%; the favourite won 53% v 42% said), under 50 points 19% (28 of 147). Women's under 25 points 35% (19 of 54, model 25%), under 50 points 28%. Don't call draws; label favourites under 50% "too close to call". The likeliest exact score in an even game can still be a draw (2-2 at 2.4 v 2.4 xG), which the "likeliest score for the pick" rule hides. `simulate.py --table` now prints the projected final Phase 1 table (average points, 10th-90th percentile range, GD).
- **Club-level home/away "personalities" are noise (`analysis/home_away_splits.py`, 2026-10-20; `reports/home_away_splits_2026-10-20.txt`).** All national leagues, 2023/24-2025/26 + 2026/27 so far, each side's home PPG minus away PPG measured against its tier/gender/season norm (raw splits just reward playing below the Prem). Year-on-year correlation of a side's split: **+0.08, +0.01, -0.05**. Sides with the same direction all three seasons and a pooled excess of 0.5+ ppg: **4 home-strong, 6 away-strong, against 6 and 6 expected by chance** (2,000 venue shuffles). Most eye-catching anyway: Chichester M (Conf West, home 7-1-1 v away 2-5-2 in 2025/26, +0.6 to +1.0 above norm every season) and Clifton Robinsons M; away side Loughborough Students W (Prem, home 1-1-6 v away 4-2-2 in 2023/24). 2026/27 agrees in 5 of 10, a coin flip on 2-3 home games each. Don't publish "fortress" claims as a trait; one-season splits are fine as a stat, not a prediction.

  **Wider re-tune, 2026-10-17: the current settings stand.** Every setting swept over a much wider range (k 4–64, mov 0–4, carry 0.4–1.0, promoted 0 to −250, home 0–120), then jointly re-tuned on 2022/23–2024/25 pooled (354 matches per gender, not one season) and tested once on 2025/26. Men's re-tune (k 24, mov 2, carry 1.0, promoted −120, home 30) beat the current settings on the seasons it was tuned on (log-loss 0.658 → 0.643) but was **worse on 2025/26** (0.761 → 0.786): overfitting, so not adopted. Women's re-tune moved nothing beyond noise either way. The 538-style margin-v-gap correction gained nothing in either gender. 2025/26 is the hardest test season because it had the most draws (men's 18.6% v 10–14% in 2022–25), and a draw is always a miss. Also added: **neutral venues** (Finals Weekend gets no home edge unless a finalist's own club hosts it, i.e. Surbiton in 2023/24 and 2025/26), a correctness fix that moved no rating by more than a point. The sweep script was a one-off in the session scratchpad, not in the repo.

  Knockouts are where it fails outright — 50% in both genders at Finals Weekend and the men's log-loss (1.405) is worse than a flat 1-in-3 guess (1.099). **Decision: publish rankings, not knockout predictions.** Why each phase behaves as it does is in [`modelling.md`](modelling.md).
- **Phase 1 simulator (`analysis/simulate.py`, 2026-10-17)** - plays the rest of Phase 1 out 20,000 times and gives each side P(1st), P(Top 6), average finishing position and the full position distribution (`--csv`). Results come from the power rankings (Elo + ordered logit, the better-calibrated model); scorelines from the Poisson model's grid restricted to that result (needed for goal difference); ratings update inside each simulated season. **Table order is points, then GD, then goals scored**: this picked the real Top 6 in all four seasons 2022/23-2025/26, both genders. **Backtest** (`--backtest`; `reports/phase1_sim_backtest_2026-10-17.txt`), P(Top 6) Brier score v "whoever is top six now": men 0.069/0.048/0.041/0.017 at rounds 0/3/6/9 v 0.250/0.125/0.083/0.042; women 0.137/0.129/0.096/0.020 v 0.250/0.250/0.167/0.000 (the women's top six after round 9 never changed in four seasons). Calibration holds: 95%+ calls came true 117 of 117, under 5% calls 1 of 86. Mildly in-sample, since both models' settings were tuned on 2024/25. First live run, before Round 4: `reports/phase1_odds_{men,women}_2026-10-17.txt`. After round 6 (2026-10-30, `reports/phase1_odds_{M,W}_2026-10-30.txt`): already near-settled, Men's/Open top three 100% and the next three 87-90% (Reading 18% the best outside); Women's top four 99.6%+, East Grinstead 87% and Birmingham 84%, the model's top six identical to the table. Men's model disagrees with the table on two places: Reading (5th) and Exeter (6th) at 18% and 4%, H&W (7th) and Holcombe (8th) at 87% and 90%. After round 7 (2026-11-01, `reports/phase1_odds_{M,W}_2026-11-01.txt`): Men's/Open Holcombe 93%, H&W 89%, East Grinstead 84% (3rd-equal on 13 pts but the weakest rating of the six and the hardest bubble run-in: Old Georgians and Holcombe away), Reading 24% (game in hand v Wimbledon), Surbiton 84% / Wimbledon 16% for 1st; Women's top six all 96%+ (Birmingham lowest, hardest run-in: East Grinstead, Wimbledon, Surbiton), Reading 7%. **The odds already price fixture difficulty**: each remaining game is simulated against the actual opponent's rating, so a separate "fixture difficulty" view only explains the odds, it doesn't add to them. **Phase 1 only**: the Phase 2 fixtures don't exist until the split, so top-8 (finals) and relegation odds need the Phase 2 round-robin generated in the simulator (venues unknown); not built.
- **Do missing regulars explain Elo's misses? Barely (2026-10-17, ad-hoc; script was in the session scratchpad).** Prem 2023/24-2026/27, 294 matches per gender where both sides had a squad and 5+ earlier squads. "Core" = in 70%+ of the team's previous 10 squads (about 14 players; sides typically miss 2-2.5 of them). Against Elo's pre-match expectation: **men's sides do 0.016 worse per extra core player missing** (on the 0-1 result scale, roughly 11 Elo points; 95% CI -0.035 to +0.003, so not clearly real), and when one side was missing 3+ more core players than the other (57 matches) it did 0.035 worse than expected. **Women's: nothing** (+0.008, CI -0.015 to +0.031). Two reasons it's weak: squads are only published after the match, so even a real effect could only soften rating updates, not inform the prediction; and clubs rest players against weak opposition, which hides some of the effect. **Not built into the model.** Revisit with a full extra season of squads, or if team sheets ever appear before kick-off. Squads stay valuable for content (appearances, continuity, with/without).
- **Squad rotation did not decide the 2025/26 table (2026-10-19, `content/series/build-squad-rotation.py`).** Prem Phases 1+2, 16 games per team, a player counted once named in a squad (rolling subs). Players used against points per game: **r -0.11 men, -0.02 women**. Surbiton men won the league with 22 players, the same as Holcombe (5th); H&W men used the most, 28, and finished 4th; Durham women used the fewest, 19. **When new players arrive:** mostly matches 2-5, then flat through October and November, then a jump in the first game after the winter break (11 new players across the men's Prem, 14 across the women's, against 4 and 5 the game before). H&W men climbed 16 to 28 by November; Wimbledon men's 22 were all in by match 6. 2026/27 after four rounds: Surbiton women have named the same 16 every game, and the women's link is strong (r -0.72) but on four games it may just be losing sides changing the team. Gotchas: order any table by the bracket, not points (docs/data-scope.md); a few squads list 14-15 names (H&W men's first game had 15), so 16 is the usual floor, not a hard one; count unnamed places as one player per shirt number. - **Cards v league position, non-Prem (ad-hoc SQL, 2026-10-15)** - built for the two Division 1 drafts (Hub p17 *Does Discipline Pay?*, p18 *The Card Table*; builders `content/series/build-does-discipline-pay.py` and `build-the-card-table.py`). Scope: all 12 Division 1 + Conference leagues, 2024/25 + 2025/26, 240 team-seasons; every league is a 10-team double round-robin, so schedules are identical within a league and "cards drawn from the opposition" is a fair team measure here (it is not in the Prem, see the Big 5 split in Data scope). **Cards do not predict position:** cards per game v points per game, z-scored within league, r = +0.09 (men +0.02, women +0.16); top-three sides average 1.37 cards a game, bottom three 1.30; of 24 league winners 4 were their league's most-carded side and 3 the cleanest. **But card rate is a stable team trait:** season-to-season r = 0.59 received, 0.54 drawn, against 0.47 goals for, 0.48 goals against, 0.35 points. Received and drawn are near-independent (r = +0.15). **One Division 1 season is too small to generalise from:** within Div 1 the link swings, Men's/Open r = +0.47 in 2024/25 then -0.35 in 2025/26, Women's +0.17 then +0.11. Women's non-Prem cards run lower than men's (0.89-1.30 v 1.30-1.62 per team per game by league in 2025/26), so never pool genders on a cards axis. **North v South:** Division 1 North had more cards in 2025/26 (Men's/Open 261 v 234, Women's 234 v 175) but the two were level in 2024/25; only Men's North yellows led both seasons (0.54 v 0.41, then 0.42 v 0.34 a game). Umpires are regional too, so say "more cards in the North", never "dirtier"; `match_officials` covers all 360 Div 1 matches if that is ever tested.
